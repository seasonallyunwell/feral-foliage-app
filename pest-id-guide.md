## Fungus Gnats
**Threat level:** 🟡 Annoying but usually not fatal

### What they look like
- Tiny black flies (~2mm) hovering around soil
- Larvae are white/translucent worms in soil

### Why you have them
- Overwatering (they breed in wet soil)
- Organic matter in soil

### Treatment ladder
1. **Let soil dry out completely** between waterings (free)
2. **Yellow sticky traps** — catch adults immediately

4. **Bottom watering only** for 2-3 weeks

## Spider Mites
**Threat level:** 🔴 Can kill plants fast

### What they look like
- Microscopic (need a loupe to see clearly)
- Fine webbing on leaf undersides
- Leaves look dusty/stippled, then yellow and drop

### Why you have them
- Dry air (they love low humidity)
- Brought in on new plants
- Spread from nearby infested plants

### Treatment ladder
1. **Isolate immediately** — they spread fast
2. **Shower the plant** — knock them off with water
3. **Increase humidity** around affected plants

## Mealybugs
**Threat level:** 🟠 Persistent and gross

### What they look like
- White, cottony masses in leaf joints
- Slow-moving, soft-bodied bugs under the fluff
- Can also infest roots (check when repotting)

### Why you have them
- Came in on a new plant
- Spread from nearby plants
- Can live in soil and re-emerge

### Treatment ladder
1. **Isolate the plant**
2. **Manual removal** — dab with 70% isopropyl alcohol on a cotton swab (tedious but effective)
3. **Insecticidal soap** — spray thoroughly, repeat weekly

## Aphids
**Threat level:** 🟡 Common and manageable

### What they look like
- Small, soft-bodied bugs in clusters
- Colors: green, black, white, or pink
- Often on new growth and flower buds
- Leave sticky "honeydew" residue

### Why you have them
- Flew in from outside
- Came on a new plant
- Attracted to stressed or over-fertilized plants

### Treatment ladder
1. **Blast with water** — knock them off (works surprisingly well)
2. **Insecticidal soap** — 2-3 applications, 5 days apart
   - https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000BQL8UY?tag=feralfoliage-20
3. **Neem oil** — preventive/ongoing
4. **Introduce ladybugs** (outdoor plants)

## Scale
**Threat level:** 🟠 Sneaky and hard to spot

### What they look like
- Brown/tan bumps that don't move
- Waxy or armored shell
- Often on stems and leaf veins
- Leave sticky honeydew

### Why you have them
- Came on a new plant
- Spread from nearby plants

### Treatment ladder
1. **Manual removal** — scrape off with fingernail or soft brush
2. **Alcohol swab** — wipe down affected areas
3. **Horticultural oil** — smothers them
4. **Systemic granules** — for heavy infestations (ornamentals only)
   - https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000BX1HKI?tag=feralfoliage-20

## Thrips
**Threat level:** 🟠 Small but destructive

### What they look like
- Tiny (1-2mm), slender insects
- Silver/bronze streaks on leaves
- Black dots (frass) on leaves
- Often hide in flowers

### Why you have them
- Flew in through windows
- Came on cut flowers or new plants

### Treatment ladder
1. **Remove affected leaves/flowers**
2. **Blue sticky traps** (thrips prefer blue)
3. **Insecticidal soap** — thorough coverage, repeat applications
4. **Spinosad** — organic insecticide effective on thrips
5. **Systemic granules** — for persistent infestations

## General Prevention Tips

1. **Quarantine new plants** for 2-3 weeks before adding to your collection
2. **Inspect regularly** — check leaf undersides, joints, and soil surface weekly
3. **Keep humidity appropriate** — spider mites love dry air
4. **Don't overwater** — fungus gnats breed in wet soil
5. **Clean your tools** — don't spread pests between plants
6. **Remove dead leaves** — they harbor pests
