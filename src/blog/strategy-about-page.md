# Strategy: Feral Foliage About Page

<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;color:#a7a7a7">Strategic recommendations for page structure, key messages, trust signals, and differentiation.</p>

---

## 1. Page Structure

### Proposed H2/H3 Hierarchy

```
H1: About Feral Foliage

H2: Plant Care for People Who Kill Plants
    → H3: (One-line hook that sets the tone)
    
H2: How This Started
    → H3: The origin story, founder's journey
    → H3: What prompted creating FF
    
H2: Our Philosophy
    → H3: Why we exist
    → H3: What makes our approach different
    → H3: Affiliate disclosure
    
H2: What We Offer
    → H3: Number of guides/resources
    → H3: What readers can find here
    
H2: Let's Keep This Honest
    → H3: Contact info
    → H3: Update frequency/commitment
    → H3: Social proof (if available)
```

### Structural Rationale

- **Lead with personality** — First section is the hook, not a generic "welcome"
- **Story second** — Origin story builds connection before pitching value
- **Philosophy before offerings** — Establishes credibility and differentiation early
- **Transparency closing** — Contact info and disclosure at end signals trustworthiness

---

## 2. Key Messages

### 3-5 Core Messages

1. **"We kill plants too. Here's what we've learned."**
   - The foundational FF message. Normalizes failure, establishes honesty, and differentiates from preachy plant experts.

2. **"Practical advice, no gatekeeping."**
   - Counter-positioning against competitors who use jargon or pretend plants are foolproof.

3. **"70+ guides written by someone who learned the hard way."**
   - Establishes experience through volume and vulnerability (not claiming expertise).

4. **"This site exists because Google couldn't give honest answers."**
   - Origin justification — why FF was created, differentiation from search results.

5. **"We're transparent about what makes money."**
   - Affiliate disclosure upfront — builds trust through honesty about monetization.

## 3. Trust Signals & E-E-A-T

### E-E-A-T Implementation

| Factor | What to Include | Priority |
|--------|-----------------|----------|
| **Experience** | Founder's plant journey (failures + learnings), years in space, number of plants killed | High |
| **Expertise** | 70+ guides published, specific plant knowledge, troubleshooting experience | High |
| **Authoritativeness** | Site age, community engagement (if any), linking from other plant sites | Medium |
| **Trustworthiness** | Contact email, affiliate disclosure, content update dates, honest tone | Critical |

### 3+ Specific Trust-Building Recommendations

1. **Include real contact info** — A working email address signals legitimacy. Even a Gmail for a solo project is fine if paired with consistent content.

2. **Add content freshness dates** — Update the About page annually with "Last updated: [YEAR]" and note any site milestones (e.g., "This site has been helping people kill fewer plants since 2024").

3. **Disclose affiliate relationships prominently** — Put it in the Philosophy section, not hidden at the bottom. "This site earns commissions from recommended products. We only recommend things we'd actually buy."

4. **Link to 2-3 cornerstone articles** — Demonstrates expertise and gives readers immediate value. Links to "watering-guide" and "plant-er" show depth.

5. **Admit what you don't know** — "I'm not a botanist, just someone who's killed enough plants to figure out what works." This builds trust more than fake credentials.

## 4. Differentiation

### How FF Should Position Against Competitors

| Competitor | Their Angle | FF's Counter-Position |
|------------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Patch Plants** | Scale, awards, "first UK online plant brand" | Not a store — we don't ship plants, we help you keep yours alive |
| **OurHousePlants** | "~50 years collective experience" | Experience through failure, not tenure |
| **The Sill** | Mission-driven, "Plants Make People Happy®" | Skip the aspiration — let's just not kill anything this month |
| **HousePlantJournal** | (Implied expertise through dense content) | More accessible, less intimidating, honest about what's hard |

### 2+ Ways FF Differentiates

1. **The Failure-First Story** — No competitor leads with how many plants they've killed. FF should. "I killed my first twelve houseplants before I figured out why." This is unique positioning that aligns with the brand voice.

2. **Anti-Gatekeeping Philosophy** — Competitors implicitly position themselves as experts teaching beginners. FF positions as "fellow survivors sharing notes." The tone is peer-to-peer, not teacher-to-student.

3. **Triage Focus** — Most plant sites are about buying/choosing/displaying. FF should own the "something's wrong, fix it" space — troubleshooting guides, root rot rescue, pest eradication. This is underserved and matches FF's voice.

## 5. Target Audience

### Who This Page Speaks To

**Primary Audience:** Adults who've killed houseplants and feel guilty/embarrassed about it. They've likely tried "easy" plants that died anyway and are tired of generic advice like "just water when dry."

**Secondary Audience:** Budget-conscious plant parents who want practical advice over aesthetic inspiration. They're not here to build a "plant collection" — they just want one or two plants that survive.

**Tertiary Audience:** SEO-driven searchers landing on "why is my [plant] dying" who want to know who's behind the advice before trusting it.

### What They Need to Hear

1. **"You're not alone"** — Normalize plant death. Most people kill plants. The About page should make them feel seen, not judged.

2. **"This person gets it"** — The voice should feel like someone who's been there, not a corporate brand.

3. **"You can trust this"** — Not through fake credentials, but through transparency: honest about failures, honest about what makes money, honest about what works.

4. **"There's a path forward"** — Not "become a plant expert" but "keep fewer plants alive longer." Practical, low-pressure.

## 6. Brand Story Angle Recommendations

### Suggested Origin Story Approach

**Lead with the failure, not the success.** Most About pages follow "I loved plants, studied plants, now I help you." FF should be:

> "I killed my first twelve houseplants before I figured out why. The twelfth was a fiddle leaf fig — the 'easy' one everyone recommends. After that, I got obsessed with figuring out what actually works versus what people just repeat online. This site is where I put what I learned."

This works because:
- First-hand experience (E-E-A-T)
- Specific detail (12 plants, fiddle leaf fig)
- Problem-solution narrative
- No fake credentials needed

### Author/Team Credibility Section

Keep it simple:

> "One person, too many dead plants, and a stubborn refusal to accept that I can't keep anything alive. I've written 70+ guides trying to figure out where I went wrong — and how you can avoid the same mistakes."

If there's a team later, expand. For now, solo founder energy works with the brand voice.

### Social Proof Elements (If Available)

- Site age: "Here since 2024"
- Content volume: "70+ guides and counting"
- If testimonials exist: One or two brief reader quotes
- If not: Skip — don't fake it

## 7. Voice Alignment Notes

### How to Maintain FF's Brand Voice in the About Page

| Voice Trait | About Page Application |
|-------------|----------------------|
| **Practical** | No aspirational language — focus on "what actually works" |
| **Slightly snarky** | "The phrase 'just water when you remember' is not a care instruction" |
| **No fluff** | Skip corporate mission speak — get to the point |
| **Honest about failure** | Lead with plants killed, not plants saved |
| **Direct** | "Here's what I learned" beats "I'm passionate about plants" |

### Example Hook Options

- "We've killed enough plants to write a guide about not killing them."
- "The ZZ plant is what happens when a plant decides to stop needing you. We relate."
- "Overwatering kills more plants than underwatering. It's not even close."

Pick the one that feels most natural for the actual voice of the site.
