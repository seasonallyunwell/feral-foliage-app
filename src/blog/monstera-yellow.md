# Why Is My Monstera Turning Yellow?

<p style="margin:0 0 16px 0;color:#a7a7a7">What it means when your monstera's leaves turn yellow — and how to fix it</p>

<div class="callout">
<strong>Quick Answer:</strong> Yellow leaves on monsteras are most commonly caused by overwatering. Check soil moisture first — if it's soggy, let it dry out. If it's dry, water more consistently. Other causes include underwatering, too much direct light, nutrient deficiency, or natural leaf aging.
</div>

## The Diagnosis Flow

Not sure what's causing the yellow? Answer these three questions:

1. **Is the soil soggy or wet more than 1 inch deep?** → **Overwatering** (Root Rot Risk)
2. **Is the soil completely dry and is the pot lightweight?** → **Underwatering**
3. **Is the yellowing on new growth or old growth?** → **Old = Natural Aging** | **New = Problem**

If the soil is moist but not wet/dry, and yellowing is spreading, check light and nutrient sections below.

---

## Symptom: Yellow Leaves Starting from Bottom

### Cause: Natural Aging
Monstera leaves don't last forever. Lower leaves will eventually yellow and die as the plant matures. This is **completely normal** if:
- Only 1-2 leaves are affected at a time
- Yellowing starts at the edges and progresses inward
- The rest of the plant looks healthy
- New leaves are emerging

**Fix:** Simply prune the yellow leaf with clean scissors. If it's not fully brown and crispy yet, you can leave it until it fully dies back (the plant will reabsorb some nutrients).

<div class="callout tip">
<strong>Normal aging vs. problems:</strong> A single yellow leaf every few months is normal. Multiple yellow leaves at once, or yellowing on new growth, signals a problem.
</div>

## Symptom: Yellow Leaves, Soggy Soil

### Cause: Overwatering (Root Rot)
This is the **#1 cause of yellow leaves** on monsteras. When soil stays too wet for too long, roots suffocate and rot. Rotting roots can't deliver water and nutrients to the leaves, causing them to yellow and drop.

Signs it's overwatering:
- Soil feels wet 1-2 inches down even a week after watering
- Pot feels unusually heavy
- Yellowing often starts on lower, inner leaves first
- Leaves may look slightly mushy or limp even though soil is wet
- Musty or rotten smell from soil

**Fix:**

1. **Check the roots.** Take the plant out of the pot. Healthy roots are white and firm. Rotting roots are brown, mushy, and smell bad.
2. **Trim bad roots.** Cut away any mushy, brown roots with clean scissors.
3. **Refresh soil.** Use a well-draining potting mix (50% soil, 50% orchid bark/perlite).
4. **Repot.** Place back in the pot (or a smaller pot if roots were severely damaged).
5. **Adjust watering.** Only water when the top 2-3 inches of soil are dry. Stick your finger in to check.

<div class="callout warning">
<strong>Severe root rot:</strong> If most roots are mushy, the plant may not survive. Cut away all rot, repot in dry soil, and hope for the best. You can also try propagating any healthy stems as a backup.
</div>

### Prevention
- Always use a pot with drainage holes.
- Use a chunky, well-draining soil mix.
- Water only when the top 2-3 inches of soil are dry.
- Empty saucers after watering — never let the pot sit in standing water.

## Symptom: Yellow Leaves, Dry Soil, Drooping

### Cause: Underwatering
When a monstera doesn't get enough water, it conserves resources by yellowing older leaves first. The plant is stressed and dropping "unnecessary" leaves to survive.

Signs it's underwatering:
- Soil is bone dry, even below the surface
- Pot feels very lightweight when lifted
- Leaves look droopy or limp (but perk up after watering)
- Yellowing often accompanied by brown, crispy edges
- Soil pulls away from the sides of the pot

**Fix:**

1. **Water thoroughly.** Give the plant a deep drink until water flows out the drainage holes.
2. **Soak it.** If soil is extremely dry and repelling water, place the pot in a bowl of water for 15-20 minutes to rehydrate from the bottom.
3. **Establish a schedule.** Check soil every 5-7 days. Water when the top 2-3 inches are dry.
4. **Consider the pot type.** Terracotta pots dry out faster than plastic; adjust accordingly.

<div class="callout tip">
<strong>Drooping vs. yellowing:</strong> Drooping usually happens before yellowing when underwatering. If leaves droop and yellow together, it's severe dehydration.
</div>

## Symptom: Yellow Spots or Patches, Brown Tips

### Cause: Water Quality / Fertilizer Issues
Sometimes yellowing isn't about water quantity but water quality. Tap water contains salts, chlorine, and minerals that can build up in soil over time, burning roots and causing leaf yellowing.

Signs it's water/fertilizer related:
- Yellowing often appears as irregular spots or patchy discoloration
- Brown, crispy leaf tips
- White crust on the surface of the soil or sides of the pot
- Symptoms worsen after fertilizing

**Fix:**

1. **Flush the soil.** Run water through the pot for 2-3 minutes to leach out built-up salts. Let it drain completely.
2. **Switch to filtered or distilled water.** Monsteras are sensitive to fluoride and chlorine.
3. **Reduce fertilizer.** During fall and winter, skip fertilizer entirely. In growing season, dilute to half strength.
4. **Repot if severe.** If buildup is extreme, repot with fresh soil.

<div class="callout warning">
<strong>Less is more:</strong> It's easier to fix an under-fertilized plant than one suffering from fertilizer burn. When in doubt, don't fertilize.
</div>

## Symptom: Yellow Leaves, Pale or Faded Overall

### Cause: Nutrient Deficiency
Monsteras need nitrogen to maintain healthy green foliage. If a plant is root-bound, in depleted soil, or has been in the same pot for years without fertilizer, it can develop nitrogen deficiency.

Signs it's nutrient deficiency:
- General yellowing, especially of older leaves (which lose nitrogen to new growth)
- Slowed growth
- Small new leaves
- Soil looks depleted or "old"

**Fix:**

1. **Apply a balanced liquid fertilizer.** Use a 20-20-20 formula diluted to half strength during spring and summer.
2. **Or top-dress with compost.** A thin layer of worm castings or compost adds slow-release nutrients.
3. **Repot if root-bound.** If roots are circling the inside of the pot, move up one pot size with fresh soil.
4. **Establish a feeding schedule.** Fertilize monthly during active growth (spring/summer). Skip in fall/winter.

## Symptom: Yellowing on One Side or Leaves Burning

### Cause: Light Issues (Too Much or Too Little)

**Too Much Direct Sun:**
Monsteras like bright, indirect light. Direct sun burns leaves, causing yellow patches or brown scorch marks.

**Fix:** Move the plant back from the window or filter light with a sheer curtain. South and west-facing windows often need filtering.

**Too Little Light:**
In very low light, monsteras can't photosynthesize effectively. They turn yellow and leggy as they reach for light.

**Fix:** Move to a brighter spot (near an east-facing window or a few feet from a south/west window). Consider grow lights if natural light is insufficient.

---

## Symptom: Yellow Leaves with Webs or Bugs

### Cause: Pests
Spider mites, aphids, and mealybugs suck sap from leaves, causing them to yellow and weaken the plant.

Signs it's pests:
- Tiny insects visible on leaves or stems
- Webbing (spider mites)
- Sticky residue (aphids, scale)
- Yellow spots or patches where pests are feeding

**Fix:**

1. **Isolate the plant.** Quarantine to prevent spread.
2. **Spray with insecticidal soap or neem oil.** Coat tops and undersides of leaves.
3. **Repeat weekly for 3-4 weeks.** This catches newly hatched pests.
4. **Clean surrounding areas.** Wipe down leaves and nearby surfaces.
5. **Consider systemic granules.** For severe infestations.

## Summary: Quick Fix Guide

| Symptom | Most Likely Cause | Quick Fix |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|
| 1-2 bottom leaves yellowing | Natural aging | Leave or prune |
| Yellow + soggy soil | Overwatering | Let dry, repot if root rot |
| Yellow + dry soil | Underwatering | Water deeply, establish schedule |
| Yellow + brown tips | Water quality | Flush soil, use filtered water |
| Pale yellow overall | Nutrient deficiency | Fertilize or repot |
| Yellow patches/burn | Too much sun | Move to indirect light |
| Yellow + leggy growth | Too little light | Move to brighter spot |
| Yellow + bugs | Pests | Isolate and treat with neem oil |

---

## When to Worry (and When Not To)

**Don't worry if:**
- Only the oldest 1-2 leaves are yellowing
- New leaves are emerging and healthy
- Plant is otherwise growing well

**Worry if:**
- Multiple leaves yellowing at once
- Yellowing on new growth
- Leaves are wilting, mushy, or have unusual spots
- Plant growth has stopped

---

## Prevention Tips

1. **Water when dry:** Check soil every 5-7 days. Water when top 2-3 inches are dry.
2. **Drainage is key:** Never use a pot without drainage holes.
3. **Right light:** Bright, indirect light. Avoid direct hot sun.
4. **Feed in growing season:** Monthly fertilizer in spring/summer.
5. **Repot every 1-2 years:** Fresh soil prevents nutrient depletion and root binding.
6. **Rotate quarterly:** Ensures even growth and light exposure.
