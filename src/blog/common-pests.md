## Spider Mites

### Identification
- Tiny red or brown dots on leaf undersides
- Fine webbing between leaves
- Leaves look dusty or stippled
- Yellowing or browning leaves

### Treatment

**Immediate isolation:** Move plant away from others

**Shower treatment:**
1. Take plant to shower
2. Spray leaves with strong water stream
3. Focus on undersides
4. Repeat every 3-5 days for 2 weeks

**Neem oil spray:**
1. Mix neem oil with water per package directions
2. Spray all leaf surfaces (top and bottom)
3. Repeat every 5-7 days for 3-4 weeks

**Prevention:**
- Increase humidity (spider mites love dry air)
- Regularly mist or use humidifier
- Check plants weekly

## Fungus Gnats

### Identification
- Tiny flies hovering around soil
- Larvae in top 1-2 inches of soil
- Adult flies near windows
- More annoying than harmful (to humans)

### Treatment

**Sticky traps:** Yellow sticky traps catch adults

**Mosquito bits (BTI):**
1. Soak mosquito bits in water
2. Water plants with the solution
3. Kills larvae in soil
4. Repeat every 2 weeks

**Let soil dry:** Larvae need moist soil. Letting soil dry between waterings helps.

**Prevention:**
- Don't overwater
- Use mosquito bits preventively
- Remove any decaying plant matter

## Mealybugs

### Identification
- White, cottony masses in leaf joints
- Sticky residue (honeydew) on leaves
- Yellowing leaves
- Stunted growth

### Treatment

**Isolate immediately:** Very contagious

**Dab with alcohol:**
1. Dip cotton swab in 70% isopropyl alcohol
2. Dab directly on mealybugs
3. Repeat every 3-5 days

**Spray treatment:**
1. Mix 1 cup alcohol + 1 cup water + few drops dish soap
2. Spray all surfaces
3. Repeat weekly until gone

**Neem oil:** Systemic treatment helps prevent recurrence

## Scale Insects

### Identification
- Brown or tan bumps on stems and leaves
- Don't scrape off easily (they're attached)
- Sticky residue (honeydew)
- Yellowing leaves

### Treatment

**Scrape off manually:**
1. Use fingernail or credit card edge
2. Scrape gently — don't damage plant
3. Wipe with alcohol after

**Neem oil spray:**
1. Spray all surfaces thoroughly
2. Focus on stems and undersides
3. Repeat weekly

**Alcohol dab:** Same as mealybugs for individual insects

---

## Aphids

### Identification
- Small green, black, or white soft-bodied insects
- Clustered on new growth
- Sticky residue on leaves
- Distorted new leaves

### Treatment

**Blast with water:**
1. Take outside or to sink
2. Spray with strong water stream
3. Focus on undersides and new growth
4. Repeat daily for a week

**Insecticidal soap:**
1. Spray directly on aphids
2. Kills on contact
3. Repeat every 3-4 days

**Neem oil:** Works but slower than soap

---

## Thrips

### Identification
- Tiny slender insects (1-2mm)
- Silver or bronze streaks on leaves
- Black specks (droppings) on leaves
- Leaves look damaged or distorted

### Treatment

**Blue sticky traps:** Attract and catch thrips

**Systemic treatment:**
1. Neem oil spray
2. Focus on all surfaces
3. Repeat every 5-7 days

**Isolate:** Thrips fly and spread quickly

## Prevention: The Best Treatment

### Quarantine New Plants
- Keep new plants separate for 2-3 weeks
- Check for pests before adding to collection
- Most pests come from new plants

### Regular Inspection
Check plants weekly:
- Undersides of leaves
- New growth (soft and tasty)
- Stems and leaf joints
- Soil surface

### Proper Care
Healthy plants resist pests better:
- Don't overwater
- Provide appropriate light
- Fertilize during growing season
- Maintain humidity (especially in winter)

## When to Give Up

Sometimes a pest infestation is too far gone.

**Signs it's time to toss:**
- Entire plant is infested (no healthy growth left)
- Pests keep returning despite treatment
- Plant is too weak to recover
- You've tried everything for 4+ weeks

**What to do:**
1. Take healthy cuttings for propagation
2. Compost or trash the infested plant
3. Clean the pot thoroughly before reusing
4. Check all other plants carefully

---

## The Bottom Line

1. **Quarantine new plants** — most pests come from stores
2. **Check regularly** — catch problems early
3. **Act fast** — pests multiply quickly
4. **Isolate infested plants** — pests spread
5. **Be consistent** — treatments need repeating
6. **Know when to quit** — some plants aren't worth saving
