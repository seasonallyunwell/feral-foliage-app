## Understanding Pet Toxicity

**The problem:** Many common houseplants contain calcium oxalate crystals, which cause irritation when chewed.

**Symptoms of plant toxicity:**
- Drooling
- Pawing at mouth
- Vomiting
- Difficulty swallowing
- Swelling of mouth/throat

**If your pet eats a plant:**
1. Identify the plant
2. Call your vet or pet poison helpline
3. Don't induce vomiting unless instructed

## Pet-Safe Plants

### Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum)
**Light needs:** Bright indirect
**Water:** Weekly, let soil dry between
**Pet safe:** ✅ Yes
**Why it's great:** Resilient, produces babies, classic look

---

### Boston Fern (Nephrolepis exaltata)
**Light needs:** Low to bright indirect
**Water:** Keep moist, humidity-loving
**Pet safe:** ✅ Yes
**Why it's great:** Air-purifying, classic fern look
**Caveat:** Needs humidity — bathrooms are ideal

---

### Calathea (Various species)
**Light needs:** Low to medium indirect
**Water:** Use filtered water, keep moist
**Pet safe:** ✅ Yes
**Why it's great:** Gorgeous patterned leaves
**Caveat:** Sensitive to tap water and low humidity

---

### Polka Dot Plant (Hypoestes phyllostachya)
**Light needs:** Bright indirect
**Water:** Keep soil lightly moist
**Pet safe:** ✅ Yes
**Why it's great:** Colorful pink, red, or white spots

### Prayer Plant (Maranta leuconeura)

## Plants to Absolutely Avoid

| Plant | Toxicity Level | Symptoms |
|-------|----------------|----------|
| Pothos | Moderate | Drooling, vomiting, swelling |
| Philodendron | Moderate | Same as pothos |
| Snake plant | Moderate | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea |
| ZZ plant | Moderate | Oral irritation, swelling |
| Peace lily | Moderate | Drooling, vomiting, difficulty swallowing |
| Monsteras | Moderate | Drooling, oral irritation |
| Ivy (Hedera) | Moderate to severe | Breathing difficulties, coma |
| Lilies (all) | Severe for cats | Kidney failure, potentially fatal |
| Sago palm | Severe | Liver failure, potentially fatal |

## If You Have Both Pets and Plants

### Strategy 1: Elevate Everything
- Hanging plants (out of reach)
- Tall plant stands
- Wall-mounted shelves
- Bookshelf tops

### Strategy 2: Choose Compact Plants
- Small pots on high surfaces
- Desktop plants that don't trail
- Terrariums (closed, pet can't access)

### Strategy 3: Accept Some Loss
- Keep trailing plants trimmed
- Provide pet grass (wheat grass) as alternative
- Some pets just won't bother plants

### Strategy 4: Training
- Bitter spray on leaves (reapply after watering)
- Redirect pets when they show interest
- Provide pet-safe chews

## The Honest Truth

**No plant is 100% pet-proof if a determined pet wants to eat it.**

The best approach:
1. Choose from the safe list
2. Place plants strategically
3. Train and redirect
4. Know your pet (some cats won't touch plants, some will eat everything)

You can absolutely have both pets and beautiful plants. It just takes some planning.
