## Why Make Your Own Indoor Plant Soil Mix?

Here's the thing about the soil your plants come in: it's rarely good. Nursery potting mix is designed to be cheap and hold moisture for shipping, not for your Monstera to actually thrive.

**The problems you're solving:**
- **Poor drainage** → root rot city
- **Fungus gnats** → breeding ground city  
- **Compacted soil** → suffocated roots

Making your own soil mix puts you in control. You decide how chunky, how drainage-heavy, how moisture-retentive. Your plants will thank you.

## The Base Recipe: 2:1 Ratio Explained

Every custom soil mix starts here: **2 parts potting mix to 1 part amendments**.

This ratio gives you:
- Enough base nutrients and moisture retention
- Enough chunkiness for airflow to roots

### Why the 2:1 Ratio Works

The logic is simple: potting mix holds moisture and nutrients, amendments (perlite, bark) create air pockets. Roots need both — water retention AND oxygen.

Too much potting mix = soggy, compacted soil = root rot.
Too many amendments = nothing holds water = constantly dry plants.

The 2:1 hits the sweet spot for most tropical houseplants.

## Plant-Type Soil Mix Variations

One size doesn't fit all. Your Monstera wants different soil than your jade plant. Here's how to adjust:

### Aroid & Monstera Mix (Chunky)

Aroids (Monstera, Philodendron, Pothos, Anthurium) are epiphytes in the wild — they grow on trees, not in soil. They want maximum drainage and airflow.

**Recipe:**
- 1 part potting mix
- 1 part orchid bark
- ½ part perlite
- ½ part charcoal

Yes, this is chunkier than the base. Yes, you'll water more often. Your aroid will produce larger leaves and healthier roots because of it.

### Tropical Plant Mix

Alocasias, Calatheas, and Marantas want more moisture than aroids but still need drainage. Think "well-draining" not "bone dry."

**Recipe:**
- 2 parts potting mix
- 1 part perlite
- ½ part orchid bark
- ½ part coco coir

### Succulent & Cactus Mix

These plants evolved in arid environments. They want to dry out fast.

**Recipe:**
- 2 parts potting mix
- 2 parts perlite
- 1 part coarse sand

No bark needed — succulents don't need the chunkiness.

### Perlite — Why You Need It

Perlite is that white volcanic glass stuff that looks like Styrofoam. It creates air pockets in soil and prevents compaction.

<a href="[AFFILIATE_LINK_perlite-bulk]" class="product-link">Buy Perlite on Amazon</a>

Without perlite, your soil turns into a brick after a few waterings. Roots suffocate. Plants die. It's that simple.

Use 10-20% of your total mix. More for succulents, less for moisture-lovers.

### Orchid Bark for Drainage

Orchid bark is chunky — way chunkier than perlite. It creates big air pockets that last for months.

<a href="[AFFILIATE_LINK_orchid-bark]" class="product-link">Shop Orchid Bark on Amazon</a>

Bark also breaks down slowly, which means it improves soil structure over time rather than just disappearing. Aroids love it.

Use 20-30% for chunky mixes, less for standard mixes.

### Coco Coir vs Peat Moss

Both hold moisture. Both add organic matter. The difference:

- **Peat moss** — holds a lot of water, slightly acidic, not renewable
- **Coco coir** — made from coconut husks, more sustainable, better pH

<a href="[AFFILIATE_LINK_coco-coir]" class="product-link">Shop Coco Coir on Amazon</a>

We prefer coco coir because it's renewable and ships lighter (it comes compressed in bricks). Either works, but coco coir is the more responsible choice.

### Activated Charcoal

Charcoal absorbs impurities, prevents odor, and helps with drainage. It's not required but it's a nice add:

- Small amount (5-10%) prevents soil from getting funky
- Especially useful in terrariums or closed pots
- Replace every year when you repot

## Common Soil Mix Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Too much perlite** — More isn't better. You want drainage, not a rock pile that holds zero water.

2. **Skipping the mixing** — Don't just layer ingredients. Mix thoroughly or you'll get pockets of poor drainage.

3. **Using garden soil** — Indoor pots aren't garden beds. Garden soil compacts and brings pests. Don't do it.

4. **Not adjusting for plant type** — Your succulent shouldn't be in aroid mix. Your Monstera shouldn't be in succulent mix. Read the room (and the plant).

5. **Forgetting to repot** — Soil breaks down over time. Yearly repotting with fresh mix keeps roots healthy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much soil do I need for a 10-inch pot?**
Use our calculator above — it gives you exact cups based on pot diameter and plant type. Roughly 6-8 cups for a standard 10" pot.

**Can I reuse old potting soil?**
Yes, but mix it 50/50 with fresh soil and amendments. Old soil loses structure and nutrients.

**What's the cheapest soil mix?**
Espoma or FoxFarm are reliable. If budget is tight, mix Miracle-Gro with extra perlite (30-40%). Not ideal, but it'll work.

**Do I really need orchid bark?**
For aroids? Yes — it makes a huge difference. For standard tropicals? Helpful but not essential. For succulents? Skip it.

**How often should I water my custom mix?**
Less often than nursery soil. The chunkier the mix, the faster it dries. Check the top 2 inches before watering.
